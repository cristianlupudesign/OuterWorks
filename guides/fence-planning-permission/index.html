Do you need planning permission for a garden fence?

Most garden fences in England don't need planning permission: up to 2 m high, or 1 m next to a road. Here's when you do need it, and how to check.

By the Outerworks team · Ruislip, West London · Updated 8 November 2026

The short answer

  • In England you can usually put up a fence up to 2 metres high without planning permission.
  • Next to a road used by vehicles (or its pavement), the limit is 1 metre.
  • You'll need permission for a listed building, where an Article 4 direction or planning condition removes the right, or to go higher than these limits.
  • Trellis on top of a fence counts towards the height.

The basic rule

Garden fences, walls and gates in England are covered by permitted development rights, which means most don't need a planning application. A new fence is normally fine if it is:

  • no more than 2 metres high, measured from ground level, or
  • no more than 1 metre high where it sits next to a highway used by vehicles, or the footpath alongside one.

So a back garden boundary between two houses can usually go to 2 m. A front garden fence on the pavement side of a road is usually limited to 1 m.

When you do need permission

  • Going over the height limits — anything above 2 m, or above 1 m beside a road.
  • Raising an existing fence that's already over the limit.
  • Listed buildings — if your house is listed, or the fence forms a boundary with a listed neighbour.
  • Article 4 directions or planning conditions — some streets and newer estates have had these rights removed. Open-plan front gardens on new estates are a common example.
  • Conservation areas — you may need permission to take down an existing fence, wall or gate, and some conservation areas also have Article 4 directions.

Your house deeds can also contain covenants about boundaries. These are separate from planning law but can still stop you building something.

Does trellis count?

Yes. The height limit applies to the whole structure, so a 1.8 m fence with a 30 cm trellis topper is a 2.1 m fence. If you want extra screening above 2 m, planting is usually the better answer.

How to check your address

  1. Look up whether your street is in a conservation area on your council's website. Around West London that's usually Hillingdon, Harrow, Ealing, Brent, Barnet or Three Rivers.
  2. Check whether your home is listed on the National Heritage List for England.
  3. If you're on a newer estate, check your deeds and any planning conditions for open-plan or boundary restrictions.
  4. If you're still unsure, ask the council's planning team before you build.

What we do

At every survey we check the height you want against your plot — front or back, beside a road or not — and flag anything that should go to the council first. We'd rather spend five minutes checking than build something that has to come down.

This guide covers England. Rules in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland differ. It's general information, not legal advice — if in doubt, ask your local planning authority.

Quick answers

Common questions

How high can a garden fence be without planning permission?

Up to 2 metres from ground level in most back gardens in England, or 1 metre where the fence is next to a road used by vehicles or its pavement.

Does trellis count towards fence height?

Yes. Trellis fixed on top of a fence counts towards the total height, so a 1.8 m fence with 30 cm of trellis is 2.1 m.

Do I need permission for a fence in a conservation area?

Not always for a new fence within the height limits, but you may need permission to take down an existing fence, wall or gate, and some conservation areas have Article 4 directions that remove permitted development rights. Check with your council.
