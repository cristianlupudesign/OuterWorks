Which garden fence is mine? Boundaries explained

How to work out which garden fence you're responsible for, what T marks on deeds mean, and whether the 'good side' has to face your neighbour.

By the Outerworks team · Ruislip, West London · Updated 8 November 2026

The short answer

  • There's no single rule like "the left-hand fence is yours".
  • Check your deeds and title plan for T marks showing who maintains each boundary.
  • There's no law that the "good side" must face your neighbour — it's a courtesy, not a requirement.
  • If the deeds are silent, agree it with your neighbour and put it in writing.

Start with the paperwork

Your title register and title plan from HM Land Registry show the general position of your boundaries. Some deeds also mark responsibility with a "T" on one side of a boundary line, meaning the owner on that side maintains it. An "H" or a T on both sides usually means it's shared.

Title plans show general boundaries rather than the exact line, so they won't settle a dispute over a few centimetres — but they're the best place to start.

Myths worth ignoring

  • "The left-hand fence is always yours." There's no such rule.
  • "The good side has to face the neighbour." Not a legal requirement. Many people choose it as a courtesy, and it's often the neat way to finish a boundary.
  • "The post side shows who owns it." It can be a clue, but it isn't proof.

If the deeds don't say

Talk to your neighbour before any work starts. Agree who is responsible, what the new fence will be, and how the cost will be split, then write it down and both sign it. It takes ten minutes and saves a lot of trouble.

Building on the boundary

Put a new fence on your own side of the line unless your neighbour has agreed otherwise in writing. Don't fix anything to a neighbour's fence without permission, and if you're replacing a shared boundary, agree access for the day of the build.

How we help

At the survey we'll talk through where the line appears to run and suggest a word with next door where it's needed. If both neighbours want the work, we can price it so the cost can be split.

General information only, not legal advice. For a genuine boundary dispute, speak to a solicitor or a RICS boundary surveyor.

Quick answers

Common questions

How do I know which fence belongs to me?

Check your title plan and deeds from HM Land Registry for T marks showing which side maintains each boundary. If they're silent, agree it with your neighbour in writing.

Does the good side of a fence have to face the neighbour?

No. There's no legal requirement in England; facing the finished side outward is a common courtesy, not a rule.

Is the left-hand fence always mine?

No. There's no general rule about left or right — responsibility depends on your deeds or an agreement with your neighbour.
