Fence blown down in a storm? What to do next

A practical checklist after storm damage: make it safe, photograph it, check whose fence it is and what your insurance covers, then get it rebuilt properly.

By the Outerworks team · Ruislip, West London · Updated 8 November 2026

The short answer

  • Make it safe first — prop or lay down loose panels so they can't blow into anyone.
  • Photograph everything before clearing up.
  • Check whose fence it is before arranging repairs.
  • Read your policy: many home insurance policies exclude or limit storm damage to fences.

1. Make it safe

A loose panel in a gust can do real damage. Lay panels flat or prop them securely, keep children and pets away, and move anything that could be blown into a window or car. Don't climb on a leaning fence.

2. Take photos

Before you clear anything, photograph the whole run, the broken posts, the date and any damage to cars, sheds or plants. It helps with insurance, with your neighbour, and with getting an accurate quote.

3. Work out whose fence it is

Responsibility depends on who owns the boundary, not which side the fence fell. Check your deeds and title plan for "T" marks, and talk to your neighbour — see our guide on which fence is yours.

4. Check your insurance

Many home insurance policies either exclude fences and gates from storm cover or limit what they'll pay, and some only pay out if winds reached a certain strength. Read the storm and exclusions sections of your buildings policy before you assume either way.

5. Fix the cause, not just the panels

Most storm-damaged fences fail at the posts — rotted at ground level or set too shallow. Putting new panels back on old posts is the most common reason the same fence comes down again next winter. A proper repair resets or replaces the posts.

Quick answers

Common questions

Who is responsible for a fence blown down between neighbours?

Whoever owns that boundary. Check the deeds and title plan for T marks, and speak to your neighbour. If it's unclear, it's often settled by agreement and shared cost.

Does home insurance cover a fence blown down in a storm?

It depends on the policy. Many home insurance policies exclude fences and gates from storm damage or limit cover, so check your buildings policy wording.

Why did my fence blow down?

Most often the posts — rotted at ground level or not set deep enough — rather than the panels.
