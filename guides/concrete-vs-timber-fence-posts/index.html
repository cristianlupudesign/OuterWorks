Concrete or timber fence posts?

Concrete fence posts don't rot; timber posts look softer but are the usual weak point. What to choose, and why the post matters more than the panel.

By the Outerworks team · Ruislip, West London · Updated 8 November 2026

The short answer

  • Most fences fail at the posts, not the panels.
  • Concrete posts don't rot and suit panel fences; panels slot in and lift out for replacement.
  • Timber posts look more natural and suit close board, but must be pressure-treated and set properly.
  • A gravel board at the bottom keeps timber off the soil, which is where rot starts.

Why posts matter most

When a fence leans or blows over, the panels are usually fine — it's the posts that have rotted at ground level or weren't set deep enough. Replacing panels on bad posts just moves the problem along a year or two.

Concrete posts

Slotted concrete posts hold panels in a groove, usually with a concrete gravel board along the bottom.

  • Pros: they don't rot; panels can be lifted out and replaced without disturbing the post; very stable once set.
  • Cons: heavy to handle; a more utilitarian look; harder to fix boards or brackets to.

Timber posts

Pressure-treated softwood posts are the traditional choice, especially for close board fencing, which is nailed to rails fixed to the posts.

  • Pros: a natural, consistent look; easy to fix to; suit close board and slatted fences.
  • Cons: the part at ground level is exposed to wet soil and air, which is exactly where rot starts.

Getting either one to last

  • Set posts deep enough — roughly a quarter of the post's length in the ground is a common guide — in concrete.
  • Shape the top of the concrete so water runs away from the post rather than pooling against it.
  • Use a gravel board so no fence boards touch the soil.
  • Use galvanised or stainless fixings that won't rust-stain the timber.

Quick answers

Common questions

Do concrete fence posts last longer than timber?

Generally yes. Concrete posts don't rot, while timber posts tend to decay at ground level over time even when pressure-treated.

Why do fences blow down?

Usually because the posts have rotted at ground level or weren't set deep enough in concrete, rather than because the panels failed.

What is a gravel board?

A board — concrete or treated timber — along the bottom of a fence that keeps the fence boards off the soil, reducing rot.
