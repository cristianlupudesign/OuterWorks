Close board, lap panel or slatted: which fence should you choose?

A plain-English comparison of close board, lap panel and slatted fences: strength, wind, privacy, looks and lifespan, from a West London fencing team.

By the Outerworks team · Ruislip, West London · Updated 8 November 2026

The short answer

  • Close board is built on site from vertical boards on rails — the strongest and most wind-resistant of the three.
  • Lap panel uses ready-made panels of overlapping horizontal boards — quick and economical, but less sturdy in exposed spots.
  • Slatted fences use horizontal slats with small gaps — a modern look that lets wind through.
  • Whatever the fence, the posts decide how long it stands.

Close board

Close board (sometimes called featheredge) is built in place. Posts go in first, then horizontal rails, then vertical featheredge boards nailed to the rails so each one overlaps the next. A gravel board along the bottom keeps the timber off the soil.

Good for: long boundaries, exposed gardens, anywhere that takes a lot of wind, and anyone who wants a fence that can be repaired board by board.

Things to know: it takes longer to build than panels, so it usually costs more to install. It's what our Built-Right Fence uses as standard.

Lap panel

Lap panels are factory-made sections, usually 1.83 m wide, of thin horizontal boards that overlap. They slot between posts, which makes them quick to fit.

Good for: sheltered gardens, tight budgets and quick replacements.

Things to know: the thin boards can split, and a solid panel catches the wind like a sail. On concrete posts with gravel boards, a lap panel fence is far more durable than on timber posts set straight into soil.

Slatted

Slatted fences use horizontal slats — softwood, cedar or composite — with small even gaps between them.

Good for: modern gardens, screening a seating area, and spots where you want privacy without a solid wall of timber.

Things to know: the gaps let wind through, which reduces strain on the posts, but they also give a little less privacy. Cedar and composite cost more than standard softwood but weather well.

Our view

For most West London back gardens we recommend close board on concrete posts with gravel boards. It costs a bit more up front but it's the one we see still standing straight years later. If you'd like something more modern, slatted is the next best choice. We'll show you all three at the survey and name the exact materials on your quote.

Quick answers

Common questions

What is the strongest type of garden fence?

Close board fencing — vertical featheredge boards nailed to rails between solid posts — is generally the strongest and most wind-resistant domestic timber fence.

Is close board better than lap panel?

For strength and lifespan, usually yes. Close board is built on site and can be repaired board by board; lap panels are quicker and cheaper to install but less sturdy in exposed gardens.

Are slatted fences good in windy gardens?

Yes. The gaps between slats let wind pass through, which puts less strain on the posts than a solid fence.
